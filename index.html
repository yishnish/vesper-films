Making the Wire Flowers song

We used one ElevenLabs Music composition at 112 BPM to generate the song. Oskar, Wren, and Vesper each had a solo to establish their voice, then each sang the same chorus in a separate section.

We separated the vocals from the backing with Demucs and cut the three chorus takes at the bar lines. Our alignment script matched the timing of each phrase to the steadiest take, stretching phrases without changing their pitch. We panned the three voices slightly apart, added a touch of echo, and layered them over a single backing track.

Making the flower snap in It bit me

We ran Blender from the command line to animate simple 3D stand-ins for Wren’s hand and the flower. We matched their starting positions to the illustrated keyframe and animated the flower closing over her fingertip like a hinged lid. Moving the hinge forward let the lid hide the fingertip without passing through it.

Blender rendered the motion frame by frame. We combined depth on the flower’s moving fan with outlines for the hand and stem. The depth guided the flower’s change in shape, while the outlines guided the hand without imposing the stand-in’s shape. We fed the combined guide and illustrated keyframe into Wan 2.1 VACE to render the shot.

Tools used to make the films

Qwen-Image 2.1
We generated character and location references and illustrated shot keyframes. Targeted image edits let us widen Oskar’s singing shot so his knee dance was visible.
ComfyUI, Python, and Vast.ai
We ran ComfyUI workflows on rented Vast.ai GPUs. Our Python runner kept the workflow, seed, and inputs with each take so we could compare renders and recover interrupted downloads.
Wan 2.2 image-to-video and lip-sync
We animated illustrated stills and used first and last frames to direct shots such as Vesper’s light sweep. Wan 2.2 S2V drove mouth motion from dialogue in Moss and isolated singing in Wire Flowers.
Blender and Wan 2.1 VACE
We animated the flower bite with simple stand-ins in headless Blender. Wan used depth to follow the closing flower and outlines to guide Wren’s hand while preserving its drawn shape. See how we made the flower snap.
ElevenLabs, IndexTTS2, and Chatterbox
ElevenLabs supplied Candid dialogue, the Wire Flowers song, and effects such as the flower snap. We used IndexTTS2 and Chatterbox for the dialogue in Moss, choosing performances line by line.
Demucs and Rubber Band
Demucs split vocals from the backing. We aligned the timing of three separately sung chorus takes, used Rubber Band to stretch phrases while preserving pitch, then mixed the voices together. See how we made the song.
ACE-Step and Stable Audio
We used ACE-Step for musical cues and Stable Audio models for ambience and effects, including the canyon atmosphere and mechanical sounds in Moss.
FFmpeg, ffprobe, OpenCV, and NumPy
We used FFmpeg and ffprobe to cut, mix, and check the films. With OpenCV and NumPy, we patched resting mouths and Vesper’s forearm frame by frame and timed his amber eye pulse to the drumbeat.
DVC and Amazon S3
We versioned the source media and motion guides with DVC and stored them in S3 so the production workspace could be restored from the project repository.
